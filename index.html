First day in a strange house

A robot is dropped at the front door of a house it has never seen. Tell it what to do in plain English. It has to work out the layout, find things and put them where you asked, using only what it has seen so far. Meanwhile the people who live here keep moving things, closing doors and leaving stuff on the floor, and the cat gets in the way.

Hatched areas are parts of the house the robot hasn't seen yet, or hasn't looked at for a while. Hover or tap the plan to inspect a spot.

    How it works

    1. It only knows what it has seen.It sees six cells in every direction, and walls block its view. Everything else stays hatched until it looks.
    2. It plans on its own map.Routes are worked out only over floor it has actually seen, then re-planned every step as the map grows.
    3. It explores the edges.When it needs something it hasn't found, it walks to the boundary between known and unknown space.
    4. It can use hints.With room hints on, it checks bathrooms first for a towel and kitchens first for milk. Switch it off to compare.
    5. Its memory fades.Every sighting is timestamped. When something isn't where it remembers and there's nothing new to explore, it re-checks the places it hasn't looked at since you asked. Switch to "Trusts its memory" to see what happens without this.
    6. Small things need a close look.Keys, socks, the remote and the toothbrush are only noticed from three cells away, or two with poor eyesight.
    7. It copes with doors and the cat.It opens closed doors, re-routes around clutter, and waits when the cat or a locked door blocks the only way through.
    8. It reads plain English.Commands can be chained with "then". It tells you when it doesn't understand a word, or when something isn't in the house.

    A robot that trusts its memory gets things wrong

    The robot explores a new house, waits 300 steps while the household carries on, then is asked to fetch one thing. Nothing ever leaves the house, so "it isn't here" is always the wrong answer. Re-checking costs extra walking, which shows up in the step counts.

    Room hints help when the house is predictable

    300 new houses per row, one errand each. SPL compares the robot's route with the shortest route from someone who already had the floor plan: 1.0 would be a perfect score.
